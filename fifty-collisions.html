Fifty Collisions

Fifty apps, games, systems and institutions. Each one started from two or three fields that rarely meet, such as cat bonds and elliptic curves, fungi and commodity markets, or chess and Harberger taxes. The Visionary pitched and pushed each idea. The Builder grounded it, stress-tested it and recorded the result. Open any card to read the full dossier and the discussion behind it.

How innovation was scored

1–3 mildA fresh combination of today's tech. A small team could ship it in months.
4–7 boldA new category or mechanic. Buildable now, but it would surprise people.
8–10 wildReframes a field or invents an institution, and still has a first step you could build on Monday.
Anchor: OACP / liteneen.net scores 3. It's an open protocol for agents owned by different people, with signed identities, store-and-forward messaging, and tokens earned for completed work.

Method: the moderator wrote 50 seeds, each pairing two to four fields, with a target tier (about 15 mild, 20 bold and 15 wild). Idea 10, One Take, came from a later bonus round on quantum randomness and AI. It replaced Crossover, which duplicated #25 Offtaker. It then ran two Claude agents in a fixed six-turn loop: pitch, ground, mutate, stress-test, converge, record. The agents were restarted with fresh memory every five ideas and given a running catalog so they wouldn't repeat themselves. They checked facts against a trends brief researched in October 2026.
Scores are the Builder's own calibrated judgment, not market validation.
