## Bonus round, requested by the user (now idea 10, replacing the retired Crossover)
51 → 10. [bold] **Quantum randomness × AI sampling × verifiable provenance.** Every AI output is a dice roll: temperature and sampling pick one path out of a cloud of possible completions, and whoever runs the model can quietly re-roll until they like the answer. What if the dice came from real quantum hardware, through today's APIs (IBM Quantum, Quantinuum's certified-randomness work, the ANU QRNG, NIST and drand beacons), with a public record anyone can audit? Play with the double-slit idea: the possible answers sit in a "superposition", and committing to quantum entropy is the measurement that collapses one of them. Play with entanglement and Bell tests: two parties can share correlated randomness, and a Bell test can *certify* that bits weren't predictable to anyone, the operator included. **Physics honesty is mandatory.** Quantum randomness does not make a model more creative or more conscious than a good pseudo-random generator, and entanglement cannot send messages. The real value is that the randomness is unforgeable, certifiable, and provably un-cherry-picked. **Tier: bold, and buildable today** with real quantum APIs plus open-weight models, where you control the sampler.
